Questions to ask a short-stay property manager before you sign

By ProHouse · Published 6 November 2026

A management commission is only one part of choosing someone to look after your property. A useful comparison sets out who does each job, what it costs and how you will know what is happening. Take these questions into your first conversation with a short-stay manager.

In this guide
  1. What is included in the management fee?
  2. Who pays to get the apartment ready?
  3. How will guests arrive and get support?
  4. How are cleaning and maintenance decisions handled?
  5. What will I see in my owner reporting?

What is included in the management fee?

Ask for a written description of the service. Discuss listing preparation, booking channels, guest communication, arrival arrangements, pricing, cleaning coordination and maintenance reporting. Establish which tasks the manager handles directly and which involve separate suppliers.

For any quoted percentage, ask what amount it is calculated on, whether tax is included and which costs are additional. Compare a realistic example that separates accommodation income, commission, platform costs, cleaning and other agreed expenses. This makes competing proposals easier to understand.

Who pays to get the apartment ready?

Walk through photography, furniture, linen, kitchen equipment and access setup before a launch date is promised. Clarify who chooses items, who approves purchases and who owns them. An onboarding service and the physical items purchased for a property are different costs.

Ask the manager to identify what is essential for opening and what can wait. Agree the scope and budget for your particular home rather than assuming a package covers every expense. ProHouse describes its current approach on the List your home page; confirm the applicable proposal directly.

How will guests arrive and get support?

Ask how guests receive arrival instructions, when access becomes valid and how access ends after a stay. Discuss the backup plan for a lock problem, who receives maintenance reports and how urgent issues are escalated. Keep security details out of public listings.

Also ask about support hours and the difference between emergency assistance and routine enquiries. Clear expectations help you judge whether the service model suits the property and the guests you want to host.

How are cleaning and maintenance decisions handled?

Agree who checks the property between bookings, how problems are documented and when an owner is contacted. Discuss purchase approval limits, supplier invoices and what happens when work cannot wait. Ask how maintenance responsibilities involving the building or strata are identified and escalated.

Before committing, confirm which approvals, insurance arrangements and property-specific requirements need checking with the relevant authority, insurer or adviser. A manager’s marketing proposal alone should not be treated as confirmation that every home is suitable for short stays.

What will I see in my owner reporting?

Ask for an example of the owner statement and how booking income, deductions and payments are explained. Discuss how pricing decisions are reviewed and what data will be available. Treat any income forecast as an estimate with assumptions, not a guaranteed return.

Finally, read the agreement’s provisions for owner use, rate changes, ending the arrangement and transferring listing or access information. Put important answers in the written agreement. To start a ProHouse conversation, tell us about your home, its suburb, bedrooms and current setup.
