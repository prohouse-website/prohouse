Card authorisation: why you may not see a refund

By ProHouse · Published 7 November 2026

Seeing an amount disappear from your available balance can feel like you have been charged. It is understandable to look for a refund afterwards. If the amount was only an authorisation hold and was never charged, its release usually works differently: the pending hold disappears, rather than a separate refund arriving.

The short answer: check Pending, not just Refunds

If your security amount was held but never charged, you will usually not see a separate refund transaction when it is released. Instead, look for the original amount under Pending in your banking app. After your bank processes the release, that pending hold will usually disappear and the held amount becomes available to spend again.

Some banks keep a record marked “reversed”, “cancelled” or similar. The exact display depends on your bank. Check that the pending item has not changed into a completed charge.

What is a card authorisation hold?

A card authorisation, also called a pre-authorisation or security hold, temporarily reserves an amount on your card. Think of it as money set aside, rather than money paid to ProHouse.

For a hold that has not been captured as a payment, the held amount has not been transferred to ProHouse. However, you cannot spend that amount while it is reserved. On a debit card this reduces your available funds; on a credit card it reduces your available credit.

Why does it look like money has been taken?

Banking apps often show a hold as a negative amount alongside other transactions. Your available balance or available credit also falls. That can look like a payment even though the transaction is still pending.

Example only: if you have $1,000 available and a $200 hold is placed, you may see $800 available. If the full hold is released without any payment being taken, the $200 becomes available again once your bank updates it. You would not normally see an extra $200 refund entry. This example is not ProHouse’s required security amount.

Release of a hold versus refund of a payment

  • Hold released: an amount was reserved but not charged. The reservation is removed. A separate refund entry is usually not created.
  • Payment refunded: an amount was actually charged and is then returned. Your bank may show a refund credit or reverse the original payment.
  • Hold captured: some or all of the reserved amount becomes a completed payment. This is different from simply releasing the full hold.

This guide describes an uncaptured security hold, not your accommodation payment. If an amount has become a completed charge, ask us to check that specific transaction rather than assuming it is a released hold.

How to check your banking app

  1. Open the card account used for the security authorisation.
  2. Find the original amount and date in the Pending or authorisations section.
  3. After the release is processed, check whether the hold has disappeared or is marked reversed/cancelled.
  4. Check your available balance or available credit, allowing for other spending and pending transactions.
  5. If the entry has become completed or posted, contact ProHouse so we can check whether a payment was taken.

The disappearance of a pending entry alone does not prove that it was released: it can also disappear when replaced by a completed payment. Check both sections.

Still seeing the hold? Here is what to ask

A release by a business and an update in your banking app may not happen at the same time. Processing depends on your bank, card and transaction; there is no single release time for every guest.

Contact ProHouse if you need us to confirm the authorisation status for your booking. If we have confirmed release but your available funds have not updated, ask your bank: “Is this a pending authorisation hold or a completed payment, and when will the released hold stop affecting my available balance?”

You can send us your booking reference, transaction date and amount. If sharing a screenshot, hide your full card/account numbers and unrelated transactions. Never send your card security code, online banking password or one-time passcodes. Contact ProHouse if you would like help checking.
